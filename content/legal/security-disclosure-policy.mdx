# Security Disclosure Policy

Effective Date: January 1, 2026

Graphical Playground ("**GP**" or "**Graphical Playground**") values the work of independent
security researchers who help keep the GP Platform and its users safe. This Security Disclosure Policy (this "**Policy**")
describes how to report a suspected security vulnerability to GP, the scope of systems this Policy
covers, and the protections GP offers to researchers who report in good faith and in accordance
with this Policy. This Policy is the "program policy" referenced in GP's `SECURITY.md` file and at
[graphical-playground.com/security](https://graphical-playground.com/security), and is
incorporated into the
[Terms of Service](https://graphical-playground.com/legal/terms-of-service) and the
[Acceptable Use Policy](https://graphical-playground.com/legal/acceptable-use).

## 1. Scope

1.1. **In Scope**. This Policy applies to security testing of:

(a) the GP Platform, including `graphical-playground.com` and its subdomains, the marketing site,
the learner application, and the admin application;

(b) GP's backend API and authentication systems; and

(c) GP's cloud rendering and compilation infrastructure, where accessed through your own GP
account and without affecting other Customers' Content or capacity.

1.2. **Open-Source Engine**. Vulnerabilities in `gp-engine` or other public repositories under
[github.com/GraphicalPlayground](https://github.com/GraphicalPlayground) should be reported through
GitHub's private vulnerability reporting feature on the affected repository rather than under this
Policy, so that the report reaches the maintainers of that specific module directly. GP will still
honor the safe harbor in Section 4 of this Policy for good-faith research into those repositories
conducted consistently with this Policy.

1.3. **Out of Scope**. The following are not authorized under this Policy and reports concerning
them will not be treated as vulnerability disclosures:

(a) denial-of-service, distributed denial-of-service, or any testing that degrades the
availability of the Services for other users;

(b) social engineering, phishing, or physical attacks directed at GP's contributors, students, or
infrastructure providers;

(c) automated scanning at a volume that degrades Service performance, without prior written
coordination with GP at
[security@graphical-playground.com](mailto:security@graphical-playground.com);

(d) accessing, modifying, or exfiltrating another Customer's Content or account data beyond what
is strictly necessary to demonstrate a vulnerability;

(e) findings affecting Non-GP Resources or third-party Infrastructure Providers listed at
[graphical-playground.com/legal/subprocessors](https://graphical-playground.com/legal/subprocessors),
which should be reported directly to the applicable provider; and

(f) reports without a working proof of concept for issues that depend on one, such as theoretical
best-practice deviations, missing security headers with no demonstrated impact, or vulnerabilities
requiring a jailbroken or rooted device.

## 2. How to Report

2.1. **Reporting Channel**. Report a suspected vulnerability by emailing
[security@graphical-playground.com](mailto:security@graphical-playground.com) or through
[graphical-playground.com/security](https://graphical-playground.com/security). Do not create a
public GitHub issue, forum post, or social media disclosure for an unresolved vulnerability in the
GP Platform.

2.2. **What to Include**. To help GP validate and reproduce your finding quickly, include: (a) the
affected domain, endpoint, or repository; (b) a description of the vulnerability and its potential
impact; (c) step-by-step reproduction instructions or a proof of concept; and (d) any tools,
payloads, or scripts used.

2.3. **Encrypted Reports**. If your report concerns a critical vulnerability or includes sensitive
data, you may request GP's PGP public key by emailing
[security@graphical-playground.com](mailto:security@graphical-playground.com) before sending
further details.

## 3. GP's Response Process

3.1. **Acknowledgment**. GP will acknowledge receipt of a report within five (5) business days.

3.2. **Triage and Severity**. GP will assess and assign a severity to each validated report based
on factors including exploitability, impact on Customer Content or personal data, and the number
of users affected.

3.3. **Remediation Timelines**. GP targets the following timelines from validation of a report to
remediation, though actual timelines may vary based on complexity: critical severity, 15 days;
high severity, 30 days; medium severity, 60 days; low severity, 90 days. GP will keep the reporter
reasonably informed of material progress.

3.4. **Coordinated Disclosure**. GP asks that reporters keep a validated vulnerability
confidential until GP has deployed a fix or ninety (90) days have elapsed since GP's
acknowledgment under Section 3.1, whichever comes first, unless GP and the reporter agree to a
different timeline in writing. Public disclosure before remediation, or against GP's reasonable
request for additional time on a complex fix, is not protected by Section 4 of this Policy.

## 4. Safe Harbor

4.1. **Authorization**. GP authorizes security research conducted in good faith and in accordance
with this Policy as necessary access under Article 323-1 et seq. of the French Penal Code and
equivalent computer-misuse laws in the researcher's jurisdiction. GP will not pursue civil action,
report you to law enforcement, or suspend your GP account for good-faith security research that:
(a) stays within the scope defined in Section 1.1; (b) avoids the conduct listed in Section 1.3;
(c) does not exceed the minimum access needed to demonstrate the vulnerability; and (d) complies
with the reporting and confidentiality obligations in Sections 2 and 3.4.

4.2. **Third-Party Claims**. If a third party brings a claim against a researcher for conduct that
complied with this Policy, GP will take reasonable steps to make clear that the research was
authorized by GP. This Policy does not bind Infrastructure Providers or other third parties, and
GP cannot authorize testing of systems it does not control.

4.3. **No Reward Guarantee**. GP does not currently operate a paid bug bounty program. With your
permission, GP may credit you in a security acknowledgments page for a validated, responsibly
disclosed vulnerability. Recognition under this Section 4.3 is at GP's discretion and is not
compensation, employment, or a contractual entitlement.

## 5. Personal Data and Breach Notification

5.1. If a reported vulnerability involves, or GP otherwise becomes aware of, a breach of personal
data, GP will assess and, where required under Article 33 of the GDPR, notify the Commission
Nationale de l'Informatique et des Libertés (CNIL) without undue delay and, where feasible, within
seventy-two (72) hours of becoming aware of the breach. Where the breach is likely to result in a
high risk to affected individuals, GP will notify them in accordance with Article 34 of the GDPR.

5.2. Reports submitted under this Policy are handled by GP's security contributors on a
need-to-know basis and are not used for any purpose other than validating and remediating the
reported issue, consistent with GP's [Privacy Policy](https://graphical-playground.com/legal/privacy).

## 6. Changes to This Policy

6.1. GP may update this Policy from time to time. GP will post the most current version at
[graphical-playground.com/legal/security-disclosure](https://graphical-playground.com/legal/security-disclosure).
A change to this Policy does not retroactively remove the safe harbor in Section 4 for research
conducted, or a report submitted, while a prior version was in effect.

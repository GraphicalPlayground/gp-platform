Epic Games announced on November 3, 2026, that neither Blueprints nor Actors will be in Unreal Engine 6, not even in early access. Gameplay will be written in Verse, a language born in Fortnite. Here is the origin of the language, the official reasons, the developers' reactions, and the likely impact.

### 1. The Origin of Verse

Verse is Tim Sweeney's idea. Simon Peyton Jones says Sweeney has been designing it mentally for about a decade, and that his own work with Lennart Augustsson is to formalize the language. Peyton Jones, a prominent figure in Haskell, joined Epic in December 2021. The language was introduced in December 2022 alongside a research paper, and released in March 2023 with UEFN, Fortnite's editor.

Technically, Verse is a functional-logic and declarative language equipped with an effect system.

### 2. Why switch to Verse?

Epic describes Verse as a multi-paradigm language, built to last for decades and designed for a shared persistent world: the "metaverse." Its defining features include failure as a control-flow mechanism, side-effect tracking, structured concurrency, and automatic rollback of changes when an operation fails.

Epic's core argument is scalability. Every Verse function executes as a reversible transaction. Epic eventually aims for automatic distributed execution across multiple servers, though Verse remains single-threaded today.

### 3. Why Remove Blueprints?

Two terms need clarification:

* A blog post ("billet") is an article published by Epic on its official site. Here, the key post is by Marcus Wassmer, Director of Development.
* Scene Graph is the new way to build a game world: you assemble entities from components, like building blocks, all written in Verse. It replaces the Actor system, which relied on objects inheriting from one another.

**Timeline of the reversal**

* **June 17 and 22, 2026** (State of Unreal, followed by the post "The road to UE 6"): Epic promises not to force a breaking change. Actors and Blueprints will be present in the early versions of UE6, then deprecated, with conversion tools provided.
* **July 15, 2026**: A blog post by Ludus AI, a publisher of AI assistants for Unreal, still echoes this plan: Blueprints fully supported in early access, deprecated only once the Verse framework matures. This now-obsolete article illustrates the scale of the reversal. Its author sells AI tools.
* **November 3** (Unreal Fest Tokyo): Slides mark the removal ("Remove") of Blueprints, Actors, and the C++ gameplay framework. Sweeney acknowledges a "painful change" and apologizes.
* **November 19** (post "An update on Blueprints and the path to UE6"): UE6, including early access, will include neither Blueprints nor the Actor Gameplay Framework.

**The reasons given by Epic**

AI would allegedly make converting UE5 projects massively simpler, or even automatic, allowing UE6 to ship on time. Epic claims the cost of an AI task drops by a factor of 13 per year. This is Epic's own claim, with no independent verification. A Fortnite Battle Royale prototype reached roughly 3 million lines of Verse after porting over 20,000 Blueprints. Performance, memory, and architecture remain to be validated. According to IEEE Spectrum, Blueprints are stored as binary files that language models cannot read directly. Verse code, written in plain text, is directly accessible to an AI.

### 4. The Developers' Perspective

Chet Faliszek, formerly of Valve, describes Blueprints as an intermediate layer between artistic work and engineering work. Without them, people must learn Verse or rely on AI prompts. Freshly Baked Games (*Queen's Domain*) says Unreal's AI pivot is pushing them to switch engines. Blueprints were a major part of their workflow. A professional with ten years of experience highlights the speed of iteration in Blueprints, without Visual Studio or compile times, and an indie developer argues that generating code via AI is a trap, as you still have to review it to optimize or fix bugs.

A longtime Unreal developer explains that AI handles Blueprints poorly and prefers code. He used Claude himself to get started in Godot. Another engineer notes that Scene Graph's ECS style is harder to code, but an AI can help manage that complexity. A forum contributor suggests Epic wants to control its core technologies to avoid relying on third-party licenses. One question remains open: developers ask why Epic created Verse when an existing language, like C#, could have been restricted. No official answer has been identified.

### 5. Impact: What Is Settled, What Remains Open

**Settled**

* Early access in late 2027, final release 12 to 18 months later.
* UE5 projects can stay on UE5, receiving targeted features and patches. Epic is already backporting AI tools (extended Python, MCP, web platform) via the 5.9 preview.
* C++ remains in the low-level layers (rendering, physics). Sweeney says current models have seen little Verse in their training data, and that this will change by January.

**Open**

* Migration tools. Their format has not been decided.
* Accessibility. Without a visual layer, non-programmers will have to learn Verse or delegate to an AI. A game like *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* relied heavily on Blueprints, illustrating what this shift means for small teams.
* Developer retention. A developer interviewed by IEEE Spectrum thinks AI could make switching engines "trivial." Epic acknowledges that AI makes converting to other standards easier and says it does not want to prevent it.
* Verse's openness. Its license is proprietary. Its implementation is visible on GitHub, but Epic specifies it is not intended for public use at this time.

**No Plans for visual replacement**

* Unreal Source reports that in August 2025, a UEFN lead said they wanted to add a node-based interface. Epic later replied "No, no plans": several visual approaches were prototyped, none proved convincing.
* The "Save Blueprints" petition dates this response to Unreal Fest 2026 and quotes it.
* No reports from Tokyo mention a node-based replacement.
* The "Verse Canvas" mentioned on some forums is a rumor: one user asked for sources and pointed out that feedback from Unreal Fest says the exact opposite. Confusion with Unreal Canvas, a Miro-inspired collaboration feature, has been suggested as a possible explanation, without proof.

Epic Games reverses its June 2026 roadmap and drops both Blueprints and the Actor framework from Unreal Engine 6 early access included (slated for late 2027). Gameplay moves entirely to Verse and Scene Graph, with no node-based visual replacement planned. Epic bets on AI-assisted code generation and plain-text parsing to ease migration, while C++ stays at the engine's low-level layers and UE5 remains supported for existing projects. Developers split over the shift: non-programmers and small studios warn about lost iteration speed and accessibility, whereas engineers note that text-based Verse works better with LLMs and scales toward distributed execution.
